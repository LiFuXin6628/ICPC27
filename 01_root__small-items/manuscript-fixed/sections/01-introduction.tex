\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Maintainers ask what a change will affect~\cite{sillito2006questions,ko2007information}; conventional explanations describe what code currently does without improving understanding of change impact~\cite{nam2024gilt}. Tracing and explaining are distinct skills~\cite{lister2009tracing,murphy2012eipe}; predicting behavioral change under an edit is the critical skill maintenance demands~\cite{sentance2019primm}.

We introduce \emph{counterfactual explanation} as an executed contrast under a specified edit. The explanation pairs descriptive prose with two execution-checked claims: a witness with paired outcomes, and an executable predicate characterizing affected inputs.

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\columnwidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule}{\small
\textbf{New idea:} explain behavioral change via an executed witness and test-checked input predicate.
\textbf{Why new:} combines both execution checks for one intervention and separates witness validity from novelty. We evaluate reader transfer and calibration in a controlled human study.
\textbf{Most related paper:} Vashishtha et al., \emph{Executable Counterfactuals}~\cite{vashishtha2026executable}.}}
\smallskip

\textbf{Hypotheses.}
\textbf{H0} (pipeline): without execution evidence, witness/predicate errors are frequent; execution evidence mitigates them.
\textbf{H1} (instrument): change-impact queries are harder than matched descriptive ones.
\textbf{H2} (treatment): counterfactual explanations improve accuracy on \emph{unseen} change-impact tasks over code and descriptive prose. \textbf{H3} (calibration): they improve probabilistic calibration.

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) The primitive and \CoDE pipeline (\S\ref{sec:approach}). (2) Evaluation of H0 on \SNMutants{} functions across two generators and checks (\S\ref{sec:eval}). (3) A model-side probe isolating change-impact difficulty. (4) A controlled human study ($N=9$, 54 judgments) showing executed counterfactuals improve near transfer and confidence calibration on unseen edits (\S\ref{sec:agenda}).
