\section{Preliminary Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}
\textbf{RQ1 (H0):} how often do generated witnesses and predicates fail execution checks with and without evidence?
\textbf{RQ2 (H1):} how does performance differ between change-impact and matched descriptive queries?

\textbf{Corpus.} We sampled 88 HumanEval+ and 88 MBPP+ functions~\cite{chen2021humaneval,austin2021mbpp,liu2023evalplus} (mean \SMeanInputsPerFunction{} inputs; 100\% line, 96.4\% branch coverage). Of \SNFunctionsAttempted{} functions, \SNNoCandidates{} had no mutation site, \SNNoNonequivalent{} had no qualifying edit, and \SNMutants{} yielded a retained edit (\PEquivalentCandidateRate\% equivalent on $T$, \SNDegenerate\% degenerate). Median edit affected \PzMedianDifferingFraction\% of inputs. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} shows operator breakdown.

\textbf{Generators and measures.} \GenModel{} and \StrongModel{} produced witnesses for all \SNMutants{} functions. Unscorable predicates receive 0.5 for balanced accuracy and are omitted from F1. Table~\ref{tab:h0} reports bootstrap intervals and Holm-adjusted McNemar tests~\cite{mcnemar1947note}.

\textbf{RQ1: Witness validity, taxonomy, and copying.}
Categorizing Qwen's \SNMutants{} ungrounded witnesses: 28.7\% valid, 31.8\% non-differing, 23.3\% outcome mismatches, 16.3\% (21) malformed. In grounded mode, malformed dropped to 7.8\% (10). Haiku produced $<2\%$ malformed witnesses.
Raw witness error fell from \PWitGenRawA\% (U) to \PWitGenRawB\% (G), but \SWitGenCopiedPass{} grounded passes reused a shown input. Counting copies as errors, grounded error was \PWitGenStrictB\% ($p$=\SWitGenStrictPHolm), yielding only \SWitGenOwnValidG{} new valid witnesses vs.\ \SWitGenOwnValidU{} in U. Strict measures novelty beyond prompt examples.
Seeded differential testing found witnesses for 102/129 edits (79.1\%) within 10 inputs and 113/129 (87.6\%) within 50, showing the power of an execution budget.

\textbf{RQ1: Affected-input predicates.} Qwen's exact-match error fell from \PPredGenExactA\% to \PPredGenExactB\% (adjusted $p$=\SPredGenExactPHolm). Four grounded Qwen predicates returned \texttt{True} unconditionally. Haiku was exact on \SPredStrongUExactN{} of \SPredStrongN{} functions in U and 71 in G (Table~\ref{tab:h0}), giving mixed support for H0.

\textbf{Sensitivity, out-of-$T$, and analytical checks.} Removing prompt examples in U/G yielded exact counts of 32/129 and 41/129 for Qwen, and 68/72 and 71/72 for Haiku. On functions with mixed affectedness, counts were 21/104 and 27/104 (Qwen) vs.\ 53/56 and 55/56 (Haiku). Fig.~\ref{fig:strata} shows error distributions across strata.
Testing generalization on up to 50 fresh inputs strictly outside $T$, Haiku remained exact on all fresh inputs for 65/68 (U) and 64/71 (G) functions (Qwen: 17/26 and 21/38). On targeted boundary inputs, Haiku classified 362/362 threshold and 506/506 palindrome-mismatch cases correctly (Qwen: 170/362, 289/506).
Where formal methods apply~\cite{sarker2026impact,person2008dse}, symbolic analysis derives exact conditions over numerical domains. Comparing head-to-head on two benchmark edits (HumanEval+/52 threshold condition and /73 loop bounds), Haiku's predicates showed 100\% empirical agreement with analytical conditions across all 868 targeted cases, while scaling to dynamic Python structures beyond symbolic solvers.

\textbf{RQ2: Model probe.} Comparing output prediction for $P$ and diff-based prediction on the same affected input: with docstrings/comments stripped and entry points renamed to prevent memorization~\cite{yang2025reversion,lam2025codecrash}, descriptive accuracy was \PProbePonefoursDESC\% vs.\ change-impact accuracy of \PProbePonefoursCF\%. The unaffected control reached \PzProbePonefoursCFCTRL\% (Table~\ref{tab:probe}). These probe results demonstrate the inherent difficulty of change-impact prediction, motivating direct human study.
